\section{How far the method converges}
\label{app:convergence}

The error that the verdicts between rigs carry from the method itself: the router's grid and search, the forecast, the scatter of routes of nearly equal cost, and the size sweep behind the missed-goal figures (\cref{sec:res_robustness}).

\subsection{Convergence of the router}
\label{app:routing_convergence}

\stale{router_grid}{The router's grid and search are judged on the 12 departures of the \SI{140}{m} e-ammonia ship to Guadeloupe with \SI{3200}{m^2} of kite and with \SI{800}{m^2} of rotors, against the finest grid we can afford as reference: stages \SI{30}{nmi} and rows \SI{7.5}{nmi} apart with every time step halved (\cref{tab:routing_grid}). A setting is accepted when each rig's saving moves by at most 3 points from the reference and the gap between the two rigs by at most 1 point, and the cheapest accepted setting is taken. Refining the grid and the time steps together from stages \SI{240}{nmi} apart, each halving moves the savings about half as much as the one before, so the order fitted to the three coarsest grids predicts the finest within 0.15 points. Extrapolated from the three finest, the converged savings lie 1.3 to 1.8 points above the reference's for the kite and 0.6 to 0.7 for the rotors, so each error against the reference understates the true one by about that much. The grid of the results, stages \SI{45}{nmi} and rows \SI{11.25}{nmi} apart with the plan coarsening beyond the first \SI{240}{nmi}, meets this standard; stages \SI{60}{nmi} apart do not, as the kite's gap to the rotors moves by 1.2 points, and there the grid rather than the time steps carries the error. The time steps matter on the finest grid, where a stage takes less time to sail than a time bin: halving them there raises the kite's saving by 2.7 points. Searching the whole corridor at once understates the kite by 5.3 points.}

\stale{router_grid}{The bias does not shrink with the saving. Routed on VLSFO, where the rotors save 6.4 points of the cost against 20.6 on e-ammonia and the kite $-0.8$ against 13.2, the bias on their gap stays as large (\cref{sec:res_robustness}), 0.9 of its 2.0 points from one departure on which the grid of the results misses the kite's trade-wind route. The bias is therefore counted in points, the same on either fuel.}

\stale{router_grid}{A draft run routes on the grid of the results on one in six of the final run's departures, since a coarser grid would bias it: stages \SI{240}{nmi} apart on half the departures would understate the kite's saving by 4 points. A draft saving lies about the final run's with a spread of \NumDraftSpreadKite{} points for the kite, \NumDraftSpreadRotors{} for the rotors and \NumDraftSpreadGap{} for the gap between them, measured on this case.}

\stale{router_grid}{The chosen grid was checked once on every rig, for the \SI{140}{m} ship to Guadeloupe, New York and Houston and the Kamsarmax to Tubarão, 12 departures each, against stages \SI{30}{nmi} and rows \SI{7.5}{nmi} apart. Every cost saving lies within 1.7 points of it and every gap within 0.7 points, except the kite's gaps to the rotors and the wing sails to Guadeloupe, 2.0 and 1.6 points, where that grid's coarse time steps understate the kite. Every case found a passage on both grids. Bilbao, whose short crossing is checked apart, moves by at most 0.7 points in its savings and 0.8 in its gaps.}

\subsection{Forecast plans}
\label{app:forecast_studies}

\studyresult{forecast_causes,forecast_live,forecast_replanning}{Sailed at the plan's speed over each edge of about a day, the kite loses $3.1 \pm 0.7$ points more of its cost saving on the forecasts than the rotors, over 22 departures. The excess comes from the wind forecast and that held speed: a deck rig's pull falls a little when the wind is a little off, while the kite's stops below its lowest flying wind, so a speed held for a flying kite is lost when it cannot fly. A master sets the speed hour by hour on the wind met, and a ship sailed so loses the kite $0.1 \pm 0.9$ points more than the rotors, no excess clear of the error, so the forecast result is sailed that way. The order of the rigs is the same with perfect foresight and on the forecasts. Re-planning more often than once a day changes what the kite and the rotors keep within the error and may understate what the wing sails keep by up to 10 points.}

\subsection{Routing noise}
\label{sec:routing_noise}

A change of \given{2}\,\% in the tether length or the smallest turn radius should barely move a kite's cost saving. \stale{base_kite}{On three ships with kites of \SI{3200}{m^2}, \SI{1448}{m^2} and \SI{16000}{m^2}, the base and those four changes span 1.4, 0.8 and 1.5 points of cost saving and 6.6, 4.9 and 2.3 points of fuel saving, in a method study, \studyrun{base_kite}.} The router minimises cost, so routes of nearly equal cost can burn different amounts of fuel. We do not read a smaller difference between two kites as a result.

\subsection{Error of the missed-goal figures}
\label{sec:check_missed}

\stale{goal_size_data,canopy_price_data,canopy_life_data}{All limits come from one size sweep over the same \given{6} departures, around the size the class study gives each rig: half to twice for wing sails and rotors as far as they fit on deck, and a quarter to four times for the kite up to the search bound of \given{\SI{16000}{m^2}}. The best kite lies inside the sizes swept on all three ships, and so do the best deck rigs, except the wing sails on the \SI{140}{m} ship and the Neo-Panamax and the rotors on the Neo-Panamax, which are the largest that fit on deck.}

\stale{goal_size_data,canopy_price_data,canopy_life_data}{Routing again at each canopy life moves the limits little: on the Neo-Panamax on e-ammonia to New York the limit with the route held is \num{1610} flight hours and the one routed afresh at each life \num{1600}.}

\stale{goal_size_data,canopy_price_data}{On three departures of a draft, routing afresh between the base canopy price and one about half again higher moved what a more expensive canopy takes from the kite (\cref{sec:res_missed}) by 0.1 points at most, and a line through the prices from USD~112 to 375/m$^2$ gave the same within 0.1 points.}

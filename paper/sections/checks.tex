\section{Model checks}
\label{sec:checks}

These checks run with the model and say what it reproduces and where it departs from measurements.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Force chain worked by hand.} \Cref{tab:hand} follows the wind through shear and the ship's own speed to the apparent wind at the rig, and from there to the force along the course, calculated from the physics without the code. Thrust, side force and spin power agree to the digits printed, and tests hold every printed value. The massless \SI{800}{m^2} kite in \SI{12}{m/s} of apparent wind pulls \SI{64.0}{kN} by hand and in the code, and its thrust falls to nothing at an apparent wind angle of \SI{15}{\degree}, the upwind limit of its glide ratio. Taking the kite's wind at an elevation of 15 or \SI{8}{\degree} instead of \SI{25}{\degree} lowers its cost saving by \tbd{0.4 to 2.9} points on the \SI{140}{m} ship. Half rather than a quarter of the tether's drag acting at the kite changes it by \tbd{$-2.0$ to $+0.6$} points.
\item \textbf{Calm-water resistance.} The wetted surface, friction coefficient and wave-resistance coefficients of the worked example of \citet{holtrop1982} come out within 0.2\,\%, its bulb resistance within 5\,\% and its correlation allowance within 0.5\,\%.
\item \textbf{Calibrated class power.} Every standard class except the coaster needs the calm-water delivered power of its source design at its service speed and design draught, through its resistance factor $f_R$ of 0.61 to 0.99 (\cref{sec:ships}). Uncalibrated, on the worked \num{50000}~t MR tanker of \citet{man2011}, the model needs 7\,\% more power than MAN's light propeller curve at \SI{14}{kn}.
\item \textbf{Coaster power.} The \SI{85}{m} coaster gets \SIrange{700}{1500}{kW} installed. The Damen Combi Freighter 3850, \SI{89.7}{m} overall and carrying \num{3830}~t, has a \SI{1104}{kW} main engine for \SI{11.8}{kn} \citep{maritimeexecutive2022}.
\item \textbf{Added resistance.} The non-dimensional added resistance peaks between 5 and 10 in head waves and stays within $\pm2$ in following waves, inside the scatter of the model tests behind \citet{mittendorf2022}, whose head-sea values reach about 8 for full hulls and 12 for slender ones.
\item \textbf{Kite without mass.} The quasi-steady kite without mass carries the tension of Eq.~39 of \citet{loyd1980} with zero weight, at the roll angle of his Eqs.~40--41, to a relative error below $10^{-6}$.
\item \textbf{Fuel saving against operation.} A kite of \SI{125}{m^2} projected, \SI{160}{m^2} flat, on a \SI{130}{m} VLSFO ship, the size the \SI{132}{m} Beluga SkySails was first launched with \citep{wiwo2008,noaa2009}, saves \tbd{1.7}\,\% of fuel on the routed round trip to Guadeloupe\tbd{, flying 6\,\% of the time at a cost saving of $-0.3$\,\%. Read as \SI{160}{m^2} projected it saves 3.2\,\%}. The WINTECC record reports fuel savings of 5\,\% on the average route mix and 10 to 12\,\% on North Atlantic and North Pacific routes for the Beluga SkySails \citep{wintecc2009_record}, without giving the kite size. \tbd{Removing the flight drag gives 1.6\,\%, a force limit of \SI{0.9}{kN/m^2} 1.5\,\%, and free canopy wear and launches 2.2\,\%, all within the routing noise.} We routed one route, not the reported mix, and did not find what explains the gap. The model comes out below the report although its router sees the weather ahead, so this check does not bound our savings from above.
\item \textbf{Routing noise.} A change of 2\,\% in the tether length or the smallest turn radius should barely move a kite's cost saving. \tbd{At draft fidelity, on three ships with kites of \SI{3200}{m^2}, \SI{1448}{m^2} and \SI{16000}{m^2}, the base and those four changes span 1.4, 0.8 and 1.5 points of cost saving and 6.6, 4.9 and 2.3 points of fuel saving.} The router minimises cost, so routes of nearly equal cost can burn different amounts of fuel. We do not read a smaller difference between two kites as a result.
\item \textbf{Routing grid.} \tbd{With corridor stages twice and half as far apart as in the results, the cost of a \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe without a rig changes by less than 1\,\%, but the cost saving of a \SI{1600}{m^2} kite rises from 14.9 to 18.4 and 22.6\,\%, of \SI{800}{m^2} of rotors from 14.1 to 17.4 and 19.8\,\% and of \SI{1500}{m^2} of wings from 7.0 to 8.4 and 9.4\,\%. The order of the rigs does not change, but the savings are not converged at the spacing of the results (\cref{sec:model_detail}).}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Error of the missed-goal figures}
\label{sec:check_missed}

\Cref{sec:res_missed} gives what staying at today's canopy price takes from the kite on three ships. In an earlier draft on three departures the routes changing between the two prices moved these figures by 0.1 points at most. A line through the prices from USD~112 to 375/m$^2$ gave \tbd{1.6, 1.6 and 0.7} points against \tbd{1.7, 1.6 and 0.8} between the two prices. In the same draft, leaving out of maintenance the canopy and tether that wear already pays for was worth \tbd{0.3, 0.15 and 0.24} points of cost saving on the three ships at today's price.

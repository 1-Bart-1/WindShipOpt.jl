\section{Model checks}
\label{sec:checks}

These checks run with the model and say what it reproduces and where it departs from measurements.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Force chain worked by hand.} \Cref{tab:hand} follows the wind through shear with height and the ship's own speed to the apparent wind at the rig, and from there to the force along the course, by a calculation written from the physics without the code. Thrust, side force and spin power agree to 0.1\,\%, and tests hold them there. The check found one error: the kite returned its side force with the opposite sign to wings and rotors. A rig alone is unaffected, because heel and leeway read its magnitude, but a kite over rotors cancelled side force it should have added. Correcting it raises the cost of the \SI{800}{m^2} kite over \SI{400}{m^2} of rotors on the \SI{140}{m} ship by \tbd{0.0 to 0.3}\,\%. The apparent wind itself was right. Sensitivities: taking the kite's wind at an elevation of 15 or \SI{8}{\degree} instead of \SI{25}{\degree} lowers its cost saving by \tbd{0.4 to 2.9} points on the \SI{140}{m} ship, and half rather than a quarter of the tether's drag acting at the kite changes it by \tbd{$-2.0$ to $+0.6$} points.
\item \textbf{Calm-water resistance.} The resistance components of the worked example of \citet{holtrop1982}\tbd{ (source not read)} come out within 5\,\% each, and the correlation allowance within 0.5\,\%.
\item \textbf{Calibrated class power.} At its service speed and design draught every standard class except the coaster needs the calm-water delivered power of its source design to $10^{-6}$, through its resistance factor $f_R$ of 0.61 to 0.99 (\cref{sec:ships}). Uncalibrated, on the worked \num{50000}~t MR tanker of \citet{man2011} with its \SI{6.8}{m} propeller, the model needs 7\,\% more power than MAN's light propeller curve at \SI{14}{kn}.
\item \textbf{Coaster power.} The \SI{85}{m} coaster needs \SIrange{350}{800}{kW} effective power at \SI{11}{kn} and gets \SIrange{700}{1500}{kW} installed. Real \num{3500}-tonne coasters install about \SI{1.5}{MW} for \SI{12.5}{kn} \tbd{cite}.
\item \textbf{Added resistance.} The non-dimensional added resistance peaks between 5 and 10 in head waves and stays within $\pm2$ in following waves, as in the data behind \citet{mittendorf2022}.
\item \textbf{Kite without mass.} The quasi-steady kite without mass carries exactly the crosswind tension of \citet{loyd1980}\tbd{ (source not read)}, to a relative error below $10^{-6}$.
\item \tbd{\textbf{Kite against a published prediction.} Pending the full text of \citet{dadd2011}: compare the pull of a \SI{300}{m^2} kite on \SI{300}{m} of tether in \SI{6.2}{m/s} wind with their prediction.}
\item \textbf{Fuel saving against operation.} A \SI{160}{m^2} kite on a \SI{130}{m} VLSFO ship, the size that flew on cargo ships, saves 6.7\,\% of fuel on the routed round trip. The Beluga SkySails saved 5\,\% on its average route mix and 10 to 12\,\% on North Atlantic and North Pacific routes \citep{wintecc2009}. Without the flight drag the saving is the same within the routing noise, because a kite this small reaches its force limit either way. Our routed figures remain an upper bound: the router sees the weather ahead, and launch, recovery and failures cost nothing.
\item \textbf{Routing grid.} Routed at corridor resolutions from \SI{360}{nmi} down to \SI{45}{nmi} between stages, the cost per tonne-kilometre of a \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe changes by at most 0.9\,\% without a rig and 2.9\,\% with one, and the cost saving of each rig by at most 1.6 points; the order of the rigs does not change. The coarser grids are slightly optimistic for every option alike.
\end{itemize}

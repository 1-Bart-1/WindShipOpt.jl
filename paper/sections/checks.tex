\section{Model checks}
\label{sec:checks}

These checks run with the model and say what it reproduces and where it departs from measurements.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Force chain worked by hand.} \Cref{tab:hand} follows the wind through shear with height and the ship's own speed to the apparent wind at the rig, and from there to the force along the course, by a calculation written from the physics without the code. Thrust, side force and spin power agree to the digits printed, and tests hold every printed value there. For the kite the hand calculation takes the pattern table's force coefficients, in the wind's frame, at the apparent wind; the table's own size is checked at the window edge, where the massless \SI{800}{m^2} kite in \SI{12}{m/s} of apparent wind pulls \SI{64.0}{kN} by hand and in the code. Its thrust falls to nothing at an apparent wind angle of \SI{15}{\degree}, the upwind limit of its glide ratio (\cref{sec:model}). The check found one error: the kite returned its side force with the opposite sign to wings and rotors. A rig alone is unaffected, because heel and leeway read its magnitude, but a kite over rotors cancelled side force it should have added. Correcting it raises the cost of the \SI{800}{m^2} kite over \SI{400}{m^2} of rotors on the \SI{140}{m} ship by \tbd{0.0 to 0.3}\,\%. The apparent wind itself was right. Sensitivities: taking the kite's wind at an elevation of 15 or \SI{8}{\degree} instead of \SI{25}{\degree} lowers its cost saving by \tbd{0.4 to 2.9} points on the \SI{140}{m} ship, and half rather than a quarter of the tether's drag acting at the kite changes it by \tbd{$-2.0$ to $+0.6$} points.
\item \textbf{Calm-water resistance.} The wetted surface, friction coefficient and wave-resistance coefficients of the worked example of \citet{holtrop1982} come out within 0.2\,\%, its bulb resistance within 5\,\% and its correlation allowance within 0.5\,\%.
\item \textbf{Calibrated class power.} At its service speed and design draught every standard class except the coaster needs the calm-water delivered power of its source design to $10^{-6}$, through its resistance factor $f_R$ of 0.61 to 0.99 (\cref{sec:ships}). Uncalibrated, on the worked \num{50000}~t MR tanker of \citet{man2011} with its \SI{6.8}{m} propeller, the model needs 7\,\% more power than MAN's light propeller curve at \SI{14}{kn}.
\item \textbf{Coaster power.} The \SI{85}{m} coaster needs \SIrange{350}{800}{kW} effective power at \SI{11}{kn} and gets \SIrange{700}{1500}{kW} installed. A real coaster of this size lies within that range: the Damen Combi Freighter 3850, \SI{89.7}{m} overall and carrying \num{3830}~t, has a \SI{1104}{kW} main engine for \SI{11.8}{kn} \citep{maritimeexecutive2022}.
\item \textbf{Added resistance.} The non-dimensional added resistance peaks between 5 and 10 in head waves and stays within $\pm2$ in following waves, inside the scatter of the model-test data behind \citet{mittendorf2022}, whose head-sea values reach about 8 for full and 12 for slender hulls.
\item \textbf{Kite without mass.} The quasi-steady kite without mass, flying across the wind on a tether of fixed length, carries the tension of Eq.~39 of \citet{loyd1980} with zero weight, and with the roll angle at the steady state of his Eqs.~40--41, to a relative error below $10^{-6}$.
\item \textbf{Fuel saving against operation.} A kite of \SI{125}{m^2} projected, \SI{160}{m^2} flat, on a \SI{130}{m} VLSFO ship, the size the \SI{132}{m} Beluga SkySails was first launched with \citep{wiwo2008,noaa2009}, saves \tbd{1.7}\,\% of fuel on the routed round trip to Guadeloupe\tbd{, flying 6\,\% of the time at a cost saving of $-0.3$\,\%; read as \SI{160}{m^2} projected it saves 3.2\,\%}. The project record of WINTECC reports that the Beluga SkySails achieved 5\,\% fuel savings on its average route mix and 10 to 12\,\% on North Atlantic and North Pacific routes \citep{wintecc2009_record}; it gives no kite size, and a kite twice as large was planned for the same ship \citep{wiwo2008}. \tbd{Removing the flight drag gives 1.6\,\%, a force limit of \SI{0.9}{kN/m^2} 1.5\,\%, and free canopy wear and launches 2.2\,\% at 17\,\% of the time in flight, all within the routing noise of 1.0 point on this ship, so none of them explains the gap.} We routed one route, not the reported mix, and did not find what explains it. The model comes out below this report although its router sees the weather ahead, so this check does not bound our savings from above.
\item \textbf{Routing noise.} A change of 2\,\% in the tether length or the smallest turn radius should barely move a kite's saving. \tbd{At draft fidelity, on the \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe with a \SI{3200}{m^2} kite, a Kamsarmax on VLSFO to Tubarão with \SI{1448}{m^2} and a Neo-Panamax on e-methanol to New York with \SI{16000}{m^2}, the base and those four changes span 1.4, 0.8 and 1.5 points of cost saving and 6.6, 4.9 and 2.3 points of fuel saving.} The router minimises cost, so routes of nearly equal cost can burn different amounts of fuel. We do not read a smaller difference between two kites as a result.
\item \textbf{Routing grid.} \tbd{Routed with corridor stages twice and half as far apart as in the results, the cost per tonne-kilometre of a \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe without a rig changes by less than 1\,\%, but the cost saving of a \SI{1600}{m^2} kite rises from 14.9 to 18.4 and 22.6\,\%, of \SI{800}{m^2} of rotors from 14.1 to 17.4 and 19.8\,\% and of \SI{1500}{m^2} of wings from 7.0 to 8.4 and 9.4\,\%. The order of the rigs does not change, but the savings are not converged at the spacing of the results, and the coarser grid takes more from the kite than from the rotors (\cref{sec:model_detail}).}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Error of the missed-goal figures}
\label{sec:check_missed}

\Cref{sec:res_missed} gives the saving that staying at today's canopy price of USD~234/m$^2$ rather than reaching USD~150/m$^2$ takes from the kite on three ships. Part of that could be the routes changing between two prices: in an earlier draft on three departures, a line through the prices from USD~112 to 375/m$^2$ gave \tbd{1.6, 1.6 and 0.7} points against \tbd{1.7, 1.6 and 0.8} between the two prices, so the routing moved these figures by 0.1 points at most. Leaving out of maintenance the canopy and tether that wear already pays for was worth \tbd{0.3, 0.15 and 0.24} points on the three ships at today's price in an earlier draft on three departures.

\section{Model checks}
\label{sec:checks}

These checks run with the model and say what it reproduces and where it departs from measurements.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Calm-water resistance.} The resistance components of the worked example of \citet{holtrop1982}\tbd{ (source not read)} come out within 5\,\% each, and the correlation allowance within 0.5\,\%.
\item \textbf{Calibrated class power.} At its service speed and design draught every standard class except the coaster needs the calm-water delivered power of its source design to $10^{-6}$, through its resistance factor $f_R$ of 0.61 to 0.99 (\cref{sec:ships}). Uncalibrated, on the worked \num{50000}~t MR tanker of \citet{man2011} with its \SI{6.8}{m} propeller, the model needs 7\,\% more power than MAN's light propeller curve at \SI{14}{kn}.
\item \textbf{Coaster power.} The \SI{85}{m} coaster needs \SIrange{350}{800}{kW} effective power at \SI{11}{kn} and gets \SIrange{700}{1500}{kW} installed. Real \num{3500}-tonne coasters install about \SI{1.5}{MW} for \SI{12.5}{kn} \tbd{cite}.
\item \textbf{Added resistance.} The non-dimensional added resistance peaks between 5 and 10 in head waves and stays within $\pm2$ in following waves, as in the data behind \citet{mittendorf2022}.
\item \textbf{Kite without mass.} The quasi-steady kite without mass carries exactly the crosswind tension of \citet{loyd1980}\tbd{ (source not read)}, to a relative error below $10^{-6}$.
\item \tbd{\textbf{Kite against a published prediction.} Pending the full text of \citet{dadd2011}: compare the pull of a \SI{300}{m^2} kite on \SI{300}{m} of tether in \SI{6.2}{m/s} wind with their prediction.}
\item \textbf{Fuel saving against operation.} A kite of \SI{125}{m^2} projected, \SI{160}{m^2} flat, on a \SI{130}{m} VLSFO ship, the size that flew on cargo ships, saves \tbd{1.7}\,\% of fuel on the routed round trip to Guadeloupe\tbd{, flying 6\,\% of the time at a cost saving of $-0.3$\,\%; read as \SI{160}{m^2} projected it saves 3.2\,\%}. The Beluga SkySails saved 5\,\% on its average route mix and 10 to 12\,\% on North Atlantic and North Pacific routes \citep{wintecc2009}. \tbd{Removing the flight drag gives 1.6\,\%, a force limit of \SI{0.9}{kN/m^2} 1.5\,\%, and free canopy wear and launches 2.2\,\% at 17\,\% of the time in flight, all within the routing noise of 1.0 point on this ship, so none of them explains the gap.} We routed one route, not the reported mix, and did not find what explains it. The model comes out below this report although its router sees the weather ahead, so this check does not bound our savings from above.
\item \textbf{Routing noise.} A change of 2\,\% in the tether length or the smallest turn radius should barely move a kite's saving. \tbd{At draft fidelity, on the \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe with a \SI{3200}{m^2} kite, a Kamsarmax on VLSFO to Tubarão with \SI{1448}{m^2} and a Neo-Panamax on e-methanol to New York with \SI{16000}{m^2}, the base and those four changes span 1.4, 0.8 and 1.5 points of cost saving and 6.6, 4.9 and 2.3 points of fuel saving.} The router minimises cost, so routes of nearly equal cost can burn different amounts of fuel. We do not read a smaller difference between two kites as a result.
\item \textbf{Routing grid.} Routed at corridor resolutions from \SI{360}{nmi} down to \SI{45}{nmi} between stages, the cost per tonne-kilometre of a \SI{140}{m} e-methanol ship to Guadeloupe changes by at most 0.9\,\% without a rig and 2.9\,\% with one, and the cost saving of each rig by at most 1.6 points; the order of the rigs does not change. The coarser grids are slightly optimistic for every option alike.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Error of the missed-goal figures}
\label{sec:check_missed}

\Cref{sec:res_missed} gives the saving that staying at today's canopy price of USD~234/m$^2$ rather than reaching USD~150/m$^2$ takes from the kite on three ships. Part of that is the routes changing between two prices on three departures: a line through the prices from USD~112 to 375/m$^2$ gives \tbd{1.9, 1.6 and 0.7} points, and we read the difference as the error of these figures. Leaving out of maintenance the canopy and tether that wear already pays for is worth \tbd{0.3, 0.15 and 0.24} points on the three ships at today's price\tbd{, from an earlier run}.

\subsection{Error of the crash limits}
\label{sec:check_crashes}

The crash limits of \cref{tab:crashes} inherit the error of the saving. The saving carries an error of 0.4 to 1.0 points with 12 departures (\cref{sec:routing}) and more with the three of this draft. For the \SI{3200}{m^2} kite on the \SI{140}{m} ship that is at least 0.08 to 0.21 crashes a year, for the \SI{2828}{m^2} kite on the Kamsarmax 0.10 to 0.24, and for the \SI{6889}{m^2} kite on the Neo-Panamax 0.3 to 0.75. A smaller limit means none, as for the rotors' lead of 0.6 points over the \SI{1600}{m^2} kite on the \SI{140}{m} ship, 0.06 lost rotors a year.

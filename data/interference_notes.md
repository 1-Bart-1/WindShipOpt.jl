# Array interference between deck rigs (Flettner rotors, wing sails)

Sourced data on how much thrust N rotors or N wing sails on one deck deliver compared with N isolated units, and a fitted array factor κ(n, s) for each rig. Built 2026-11-01. κ = (total thrust of the array) / (n × thrust of one isolated unit at the same apparent wind). s is the centre-to-centre spacing between neighbouring units, in rotor diameters D or mean chords c. "Derived" marks a number computed here from a source's table; "fit" marks a parameter fitted here; everything else is quoted. Page numbers are PDF pages of the files in `paper/literature_pdfs/`.

The AWA-weighted κ (derived) takes the true wind angle uniform over 0–180°, the ship at 0.4 or 0.6 of the true wind speed (the two ends of each range), drops apparent wind angles below 30° where a rotor is stopped, interpolates CX linearly between the measured angles and weights each angle by V_a², so κ = Σ V_a² CX_pair / Σ V_a² CX_isolated. Tillig's "up to 38%" is the single 3D tandem point at k = 2 and 30° AWA, where CX is 0.95 against 3.9 at the beam, and his "up to 7%" is the k = 1 | 1.5 tandem on a reach. Weighted over AWA the tandem pair at 3D loses 4–6% at k = 2 and nothing at k ≤ 1.5; side by side at 3D it loses 7–9% at k = 2; at 7.5D and 15D the pair shows a 1–5% gain. Read the gain as the baseline offset rather than physics: the two-rotor rig used different balances from the single-rotor reference (p. 3), the CX error bars are ±0.1–0.15 (5–7%, Figs. 17–20), and rotor A's CL sits 4–8% above the single rotor even at 15D. Dividing by the 15D κ of about 1.02 puts the 3D tandem pair at 0.92–0.94 and the 7.5D pair at 1.00–1.03. Bordogna tests k ≤ 2 at Re = 10⁵, while a full-scale rotor runs at α ≈ 3 (CL ≈ 9.5 against 3.9 at k = 2) and Re ≈ 10⁷; the paper shows the downwash, and with it the drag rise on the downstream rotor, growing with k (p. 8), and argues only that Re matters little above k ≈ 2.5 in supercritical flow (p. 12–13). Bordogna does not give arrays of more than two, staggered layouts, side-by-side pairs beyond 3D, spacings between 7.5D and 15D, k > 2, a hull or superstructure, the interaction effect on spin power, or any tabulated number. Endo 2025 is excluded from the fit: a 50 m sail in a 100 m high domain with a slip floor means heavy blockage, and a 23–44% loss at 20D contradicts every other source. Tillig 2020 TRD (§4.2) and Lu & Ringsberg 2020 (one rotor or one wing per ship, EMSA 2023 p. 29–30) do not model rotor–rotor interaction at all. ShipCLEAN (Tillig 2020, p. 8) models the viscous wake as a 5% wind-speed reduction in a strip one diameter (or chord) wide along the potential streamlines downwind of each unit, and the superstructure wake as a sinusoidal recovery to free stream over 20 superstructure heights.

Two sources show that re-trimming each sail recovers much of the loss: van Reen's spacing optimisation lifts κ at 60° from 0.79 to 0.96, and Li et al. (2015, cited in Malmek 2024 p. 2) found +42.3% thrust with independent sheeting. Malmek and Xu hold all sails at one angle of attack, so their κ is a lower bound for an operated rig. Wang 2024 (abstract, p. 1) gives 1.5c as the minimum single-row spacing at 30°, 90° and 120°, and 4c transverse × 10c longitudinal as the best double-row layout. Mboumba's 3 sails at κ ≈ 1 and 5 sails at 0.77 stand at different spacings, 60 m and 35 m, so they fit a monotone spacing model. The thesis calls both a "distance between sails" without saying centre to centre; five 25 m chords with 35 m gaps would need 265 m on a 229 m ship, so both are read as centre to centre. Mboumba is weak evidence: the single-sail thrust coefficient changes from 5.46 to 8.71 with domain size (p. 48 Table 9) and the run uses a mid-size domain, the 3-sail κ scatters from 0.88 to 1.09 between wind angles with κ > 1 dead downwind, the thrust coefficients (up to 7.9) are not normalised by any area given, and the 25 m/s wind is far outside operation (p. 57).

## Proposed model

κ(n, s) = 1 − (1 − 1/n) · A · exp(−s/s0), clamped to κ ≤ 1. The factor (1 − 1/n) treats the most upwind unit as free and each of the other n − 1 as losing the same fraction, which is how a line of units sees a wake when only the nearest neighbour matters. A and s0 are dimensionless with s0 in D (rotors) or c (wings). The model gives the envelope-mean factor to apply to every apparent wind angle; the worst-case set applies when the wind runs along the row.

The mean fit stands on one measured spacing with a loss: at 7.5D and 15D Bordogna's pair is not distinguishable from two isolated rotors, so s0 is set by the 3D point against Han's 9D CFD, and Bordogna's k ≤ 2 is below the α ≈ 3 at which the model's rotors run, where his data suggest a stronger downwash and larger losses. The 1 − 1/n scaling to n > 2 is not tested by any rotor source.

The wing fit is weak: all three-dimensional wing data sit at s ≈ 1.9c, so s0 rests on Wang's two-dimensional 10c recommendation. Adding Mboumba's two points at equal weight pulls the fit to A = 1.31, s0 = 0.98c and raises the rms from 0.031 to 0.039, with Malmek off by +0.07 and Mboumba's 3 sails by −0.05; at a quarter weight it gives A = 1.04, s0 = 1.20c, rms 0.042. Mboumba's 3 sails at 2.4c losing nothing is the one 3D datum against a slow decay, and its quality does not carry a fit on its own, so A = 0.44, s0 = 2.86c stand, with A = 1.3, s0 = 1.0c as a joint alternative for sensitivity runs. Method scatter (0.79 in 3D RANS against 0.885 in IDDES at the same spacing) is larger than any spacing trend in the data. For a 2 × 2 or other side-by-side layout, scale the wing loss by about 1.5: van Reen's quad layout loses 0.185 against 0.109 for the line at the same 1.2c, 0.247 against 0.164 once divided by (1 − 1/n). For individually trimmed wings, multiply the loss by (1 − r) with r = 0 to 0.8; van Reen's spacing optimisation at 60° gives r = 0.81 and at 90°/120° r = 0.

Potential-flow interaction decays as 1/s, not exponentially: a unit of circulation Γ = ½·CL·w·U (w = chord or diameter) induces v/U = CL·w/(4π·s) at distance s (Kutta–Joukowski with Biot–Savart, Tillig 2020 eqs. 15–19). A rotor at CL ≈ 9 and a wing at CL ≈ 1.5–2 with 3–4 times the width carry similar lift per metre of span, so in metres their potential interaction is similar. The exponential form is an empirical fit to the viscous and wake part, which dominates the measured losses.

## Minimum physical spacing

Rotors: the end plate sets the hard floor. Kühl 2026 (p. 20) and Han 2025 (p. 3) use end-plate diameter 2D, so s > 2D. The smallest spacing with measured interaction data is 3D (Bordogna), the CFD-based design guideline is 9D (Han 2025, p. 12, limited by the 10D width of an MR tanker deck), and at 15D the measured pair is within the offset of two isolated rotors for 60–150° AWA (Bordogna 2020, p. 11, Fig. 20). Use s ≥ 3D as the model floor and 9D as the default.

Wing sails: a wing that weathervanes freely through 360° about its pivot sweeps a circle of radius max(x_p, 1 − x_p)·c, so two such wings need s ≥ 2·max(x_p, 1 − x_p)·c. For the quarter-chord pivot of Malmek and Xu that is 1.5c, matching Wang's aerodynamic minimum of 1.5c (derived here). van Reen's optimised 1.15c and 1.25c gaps only work with coordinated rotation.

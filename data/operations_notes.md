# Operational penalties of fixed rigs: air draft and cargo handling

Sourced inputs for comparing FIXED deck rigs and launch masts against RETRACTABLE ones (tilting, folding, rail-mounted, telescopic) for the kite, the wing sail and the Flettner rotor. Built 2026-11-01. "Estimate" marks a number that no source states, with the reasoning next to it; everything else is quoted or read from the source given. "Via search snippet" means the page itself could not be read. Currency 1.16 USD/EUR as in `assumptions.yaml`. Page numbers for EMSA 2023 are its printed page numbers (`paper/literature_pdfs/emsa2023.pdf`).

## 0. Main findings

- **No price for retractability is published.** No supplier, paper or press item gives the price of a Norsepower Tilting Foundation, an Anemoi rail or folding system, a WindWings or Oceanbird fold/tilt, or a telescopic kite mast, as an absolute figure or a share of the rig price. Norsepower lists the tilting foundation only as "an option" (Norsepower brochure 2025, https://www.norsepower.com/app/uploads/2025/04/Norsepower-Brochure-91.pdf); Anemoi's deployment page gives operating times but no mass or price (https://anemoimarine.com/flettner-rotor-ship/). Every premium below is an estimate, and the bottom-up reasoning is given in §4.
- **No charter-rate or asset-value discount for "restricted trading" is published for wind rigs.** The literature found prices air draft only as a feasibility question: a ship that cannot transit must deviate, re-route or be replaced (handybulk.com, _Canal restrictions in ship chartering_, https://www.handybulk.com/canal-restrictions-in-ship-chartering/). BAR Technologies names charterparty terms as a barrier for wind propulsion but gives no discount (Hellenic Shipping News, https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1129699). The penalty in §5 is therefore built from lost days times a time-charter equivalent (TCE), not from a market discount.
- **The model already prices retractability; do not add a second parameter for it.** `retractable_rig_premium` (0.12, 0.08–0.20) is the rail/folding/tilting premium for wings and rotors, and `launch_mast_telescope_factor` (2.5, low 1.2 = fixed mast with a rail carriage) is the kite's. The proposal in §5 reuses both and adds only the penalties of the fixed option.
- **`max_air_draft` (45 m) cannot be compared with rig height alone.** A fixed rig's air draft is the ballast freeboard plus foundation plus rig height, and on a bulk carrier the ballast freeboard alone is 9–18 m (§1.2). A 35 m rotor on a ballasted Kamsarmax is already ~50 m above the water. On container ships and car carriers the rig only matters where it rises above the ship's own highest point.
- **Every rotor or wing found on a bulk carrier can be moved, tilted or folded; the fixed one found is on a tanker.** Bulk: _Afros_ (longitudinal rails), _TR Lady_ (transverse rails), _Sea Zhoushan_ (five tilting Norsepower rotors), _Sohar Max_ (five folding Anemoi rotors), _Pyxis Ocean_ (folding WindWings). Fixed: _Maersk Pelican_, an LR2 tanker with two 30 × 5 m Norsepower rotors (`interference_notes.md`). Owners paying for the mechanism on every bulk carrier is the strongest evidence that a fixed rig there costs more in port than the mechanism costs to buy.

### 1.3 What a restriction costs

- **Deviation.** Kiel Canal: "distance benefits averaging 250 nautical miles"; Dover–Kiel saves 368 nm and ~18 h against Skagen, Rotterdam–Klaipėda 183 nm (Kiel Canal brochure, via search snippet, https://www.kiel-canal.de/regulations/). A ship barred from Panama goes via Suez or the Cape, which closes the trade rather than lengthening it.
- **Waiting for tide or river stage.** River clearances move with the stage (NOAA, above); a ship with a small excess can wait for a lower stage or load deeper. No published hours were found. _Estimate_: 0.5–1 day per affected call where the excess is within a metre or two.
- **Cargo lost.** Where the limit is the bridge in front of the berth (Mississippi grain, Paraná, Rouen, Houston, Philadelphia), the ship cannot take that cargo, and the owner takes a different fixture. _Estimate_: 1–3 days of TCE per lost call, for repositioning to the next-best cargo.
- **Charter discount.** None published (§0). _Estimate_: while wind ships are a small share of a class, charterers fix them on the trades they fit and the rate is not discounted; the cost shows up as lost options, which the share-of-calls times days-lost formulation in §5 captures.
- **Share of trade affected.** _Estimate_, no source counts calls by air draft. About 60 % of US soybean exports leave from Gulf terminals fed by Mississippi barges (Reuters via Grainews, Nov 2024, https://www.grainews.ca/daily/rains-swell-mississippi-river-us-soy-exports-flow-at-4-year-high), and the ship-channel elevators lie above New Orleans; with the Paraná and Seine grain and the Seaway, a Handysize-to-Kamsarmax whose ballast air draft is 45–55 m loses an estimated 3–10 % of its port calls. A Capesize or VLCC calls mostly at open-sea terminals and loses an estimated 0–2 % below the Suez limit.

## 2. Cargo handling

### 2.1 Interference by ship type

EMSA 2023 (p. 33): "WAPS should not interfere with the operability of the ship, such as the loading or unloading of cargo. On-deck cranes (e.g., on bulk carriers, general cargo ships or tankers), as well as cranes and other machinery on the dock should freely operate and should not be obstructed by the presence of WAPS." Its remedies: install on one side only, tilt or fold at the dock, or move "on a rail to either side of the ship"; and (p. 2) "a WAPS might prevent a ship from passing through bridges or it could hinder loading or unloading by cranes … These challenges are often tackled by foldable or tiltable WAPS solutions." CE Delft 2016 (§3.2, PDF p. 65): ships with a WPT may "not be able to load/unload in all ports/at all quays or sail on each route", a barrier "least relevant for towing kites" and not relevant for retractable designs.

### 2.2 Time in port

- **Retractable rigs cost minutes.** Anemoi rail: "operated by two crew members and only takes 10 minutes for the Rotor Sails to be repositioned"; folding: "less than 10 minutes to be lowered or raised"; the hydraulics run from the anchor windlass power, unused in port (https://anemoimarine.com/flettner-rotor-ship/; Lloyd's List, https://www.lloydslist.com/LL1153376/, via search snippet). _SC Connector_: tilting to almost horizontal and raising again took "less than 30 minutes in total" (Marine Log/Baird Maritime, via search snippet). Since 2018 _Afros_ has made over 40 port visits with "no port rejections or delays" (Anemoi, https://anemoimarine.com/the-future-proofed-bulk-carrier-qa/; manufacturer claim). _Pyxis Ocean_ visited six ports for eight loading and discharging operations with its wings folded (search snippet, secondary press); _SC Connector_ "has been sailing as per its schedule" (Norsepower). The kite: launch and landing take 10–20 min (SkySails) to 30 min (Airseas) (`kite_costs_notes.md`) and happen at sea.
- **Fixed rigs: no published figure.** No operator states extra port hours for a fixed rig on a bulk carrier, because none was found in service. _Estimate_: a gearless bulk carrier with N fixed units between its hatches must be warped along the berth past each unit, or the loader boom luffed over it, at ~1–2 h each; with 2–4 units this is 2–8 h a call, and at terminals with fixed-height booms the ship cannot load at all. A geared ship's own cranes lose part of their slewing arc; 1–4 h a call.

### 2.3 Value of an hour

Lost time is priced at the TCE the ship would have earned; port dues are per call (`loading_port_dues`, `discharge_port_dues`) and do not grow with an extra hour. Auxiliary fuel at berth comes on top and is small against the TCE.

## 4. Estimated premiums

- **Rotor tilt or fold.** _Estimate_: a 35 × 5 m Norsepower rotor weighs 56 t on a 24 t foundation (brochure 2025). Tilting it about a deck hinge with its centre of mass ~17 m up takes ~9 MNm (56 t × 9.81 × 17 m), i.e. two cylinders of ~1.9 MN each at a 2.5 m lever, plus hinge pins, deck locks, a power pack and a foundation ~50 % heavier. At the `cost_basis_notes.md` rates (stiffened steel 2.97 USD/kg manufacturing, market factor 2.0) the extra 10–15 t of steel costs 60–90 kUSD and the hydraulics and controls an estimated 40–80 kUSD; 100–170 kUSD on a rotor priced at ~1.0 MUSD (Norsepower 2019 line, `rig_costs_notes.md`) is +10–17 %.
- **Rotor rails.** _Estimate_: two crane rails across a 32 m deck, a bogie and a hydraulic drive and lock carry the same order of steel and hydraulics as a tilt, and the rail also needs deck strengthening along its run; +10–25 %.
- **Wing fold or tilt.** _Estimate_: the Wing 560 weighs 98.5 t without foundation (Damen), so its tilting moment is about twice the rotor's, on a unit priced 0.8–1.1 MUSD (`cost_basis_notes.md`, `rig_costs_notes.md`); the share is similar to the rotor, +10–20 %.
- **Kite telescopic mast.** Derived from the existing parameters: with `launch_mast_height_ratio` 1.15, `launch_mast_mass_coefficient` 4.1e-4, `launch_mast_steel_cost` 2.51 USD/kg and the market factor 2.0, the mast costs 9 / 28 / 74 kUSD (fixed, factor 1.2) against 19 / 58 / 153 kUSD (telescopic, 2.5) at 160 / 400 / 800 m². The premium of 10 / 30 / 80 kUSD is already in the model through `launch_mast_telescope_factor`. A fixed mast only fits a kite that always lands on it (`assumptions.yaml`).

## 5. Proposed model

Each rig is either fixed or retractable. Retractable pays its premium and no operational penalty. Fixed pays no premium and two penalties, both in lost days priced at the class TCE (§2.3):

1. **Air draft.** Fixed air draft H = max(ship's own air draft, ballast freeboard + foundation + rig height); a kite mast stands on the forecastle. If H > `air_draft_hard_limit` of the class, the fixed option is infeasible. If H > `air_draft_soft_limit`, the ship loses `air_draft_restricted_share` of its calls at `air_draft_days_per_restricted_call` each. A retractable rig is assessed at its stowed height, which is below the ship's own air draft for every system in §3.
2. **Cargo handling.** Fixed deck rigs add `fixed_rig_port_hours` per call (by ship type), or are infeasible where marked; the bow kite adds `fixed_kite_mast_port_hours`.

Annual penalty [USD] = calls_per_year × (`air_draft_restricted_share` × `air_draft_days_per_restricted_call` + `fixed_rig_port_hours` / 24) × TCE. Calls per year follow from the model's voyage and `port_time_per_call`.

### 5.3 What the model will show

_Estimate_, as a check on scale: a Kamsarmax making ~12 calls a year at 13,000 USD/day with fixed rotors between its hatches pays 12 × (0.06 × 2 + 4/24) × 13,000 ≈ 45 kUSD a year, against a retractable premium of ~0.12 × 3 × 1.0 MUSD = 360 kUSD once, or ~34 kUSD a year as an annuity at 8 % over 25 years. The two are close, so the choice is sensitive to `fixed_rig_port_hours`, the least-supported number here, and to how many fixed-boom terminals refuse the ship outright, which this formula does not count. Owners' revealed choice of rails, folding or tilting on every bulk carrier found suggests the real penalty is the high end or the refusal case. On tankers and car carriers the cargo penalty is zero and the fixed rig wins unless its air draft crosses a limit; on a car carrier it does for any 35–40 m rig (§1.2), which is why the Wing 560 tilts. On container ships a fixed deck rig is infeasible, and a fixed kite mast below the stacks costs nothing.

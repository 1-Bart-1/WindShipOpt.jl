# One cost basis for kite, wing sail and rotor: manufacturing cost × market factor

This file builds the capital cost of all three rigs in the same way. First the manufacturing cost of each rig is built up from its parts: materials and bought-in parts at purchase price, plus direct labour for fabrication and assembly. That cost is then turned into a market price with one factor, `market_price_factor`, applied the same way to all three rigs and to the consumables (canopy and tether). Installation stays a separate fraction of the market price for all three: 15 % on a newbuild, 25 % as a retrofit (EMSA 2023, Table 7). Built 2026-10-31. Currencies are converted at 1.16 USD/EUR, the rate in `assumptions.yaml`. "Estimate" marks a number that no source states, with the reasoning next to it. Prices are not escalated to a common year. They mix 2019 NREL USD, 2024–2026 company figures and 2025 wages, and the Norsepower 2019 prices used for the checks are not inflated either. The two older notes are `rig_costs_notes.md` and `kite_costs_notes.md`; this file replaces their "×1.3 / ×1.5 assembly and margin" steps.

## 0. Corrections to the requested method

- **WISDEM costs per kg are supplier prices, not manufacturing costs.** The rotor and wing in `rig_costs_notes.md` used the NREL Cost and Scaling Model coefficients (blade 14.6, tower 2.9, yaw system 8.3 USD/kg). Fingersh et al. (2006, p. 11) state how the blade figure was built: "the sum of the material costs and labor costs, divided by (1 - 0.28)", with the 28 % covering "profit, overhead, and other costs". The tower figure is likewise a WindPACT price for a finished tower. Removing the ×1.3 therefore leaves a supplier price, and multiplying by a market factor on top of that would count overhead and profit twice. The structural parts below are rebuilt from bottom-up manufacturing models instead: the NREL blade cost model for composites, and the WISDEM Farkas & Jarmai steel model for steel. WISDEM per-kg figures are kept only for bought-in machinery (bearings, gearboxes), where the rig maker really does pay a supplier price.
- **Tilting and retraction are left out of the wing's fixed cost.** The old notes put 80 kUSD for a tilt mechanism into `wing_unit_cost`, and `hatch_rig_premium` (+10 %) also prices rails, folding and tilting. Counting both doubles that cost. Neither the rotor nor the wing below includes tilting; it stays in `hatch_rig_premium`.
- **The tether is a finished bought-in product.** Putting the market factor on it gives about 158 USD/kg, which matches retail Dyneema reels (145–150 USD/kg). An operator could also buy replacement rope straight from the rope maker at about 79 USD/kg. The factor is applied anyway, as requested, but `tether_price` at 80 USD/kg in today's yaml is already a purchase price.

## 1. Market price factor

Definition: market price = manufacturing cost × F. Manufacturing cost is direct materials, bought-in parts and direct labour. F has two parts:

- **k_ind**, which turns direct cost into full production cost: production overhead, tooling, equipment, buildings, maintenance and the capital tied up in them.
- **1/(1 − GM)**, which turns production cost (COGS) into selling price. The gross margin GM pays for engineering and R&D, SG&A, sales and profit; warranty provisions usually sit inside COGS (§1.3).

So F = k_ind / (1 − GM).

### 1.3 Recommended factor

| Case | k_ind | GM   | F       | Reasoning                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---- | ----- | ---- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low  | 1.37  | 13 % | **1.6** | A mature, high-volume market like wind turbines today (Vestas 2025, Samsung Heavy 2025).                                                                                                                             |
| Base | 1.37  | 30 % | **2.0** | Low-volume marine equipment: Anemoi 21–36 %, Damodaran Shipbuilding & Marine and Electrical Equipment 27–32 %, Alfa Laval 35 %.                                                                                      |
| High | 1.37  | 45 % | **2.5** | Specialised low-volume machinery with heavy engineering amortisation (Damodaran Europe Machinery 44.7 %), the margin a WAPS maker would need to cover the R&D it now loses money on (Anemoi operating margin −30 %). |

The rotor check in §5 supports the base. Bottom-up cost × 2.0 reproduces Norsepower's 2019 prices to within −13 % to +6 %, while the rig makers' own gross margins (Anemoi 21–36 %) would imply about 1.75–2.1. Two things are not in F: the margin of the Chinese contract builders (Anemoi and Norsepower both build in China) sits inside the Western maker's COGS, and a dealer margin is not modelled because WAPS are sold direct.

### 4.5 Tether (consumable)

The kite maker buys finished Dyneema rope, so its manufacturing cost is the series purchase price: **79 USD/kg** (low 50, high 150). Base is Faggiani & Schmehl (2018), EUR 9,000 for 1,500 m of 12 mm rope; high is the retail reel price (Samson AmSteel-Blue 145–150 USD/kg); low is an estimate for large-diameter bulk rope (`kite_costs_notes.md`). No public price for Dyneema fibre per kg was found, so a fibre-plus-braiding build-up was not possible.

The paraglider check uses the same bill of materials with Vietnamese labour of 2–4 USD/h (garment wages USD 300/month, Viet Nam News 28 Aug 2024). That gives 30–70 USD per flat m², central ~45. Retail is 115–160 USD per flat m² (EN-A wings at €2,500–3,500, airetaventure.com), so retail is about 3 × the manufacturing cost. That is above F = 2.0, as it should be, because retail adds dealer margin and VAT on top of the maker's price.

## 5. Model inputs

All inputs are manufacturing cost except `market_price_factor`. The model multiplies every rig hardware and consumable cost by `market_price_factor`, then adds installation at `*_installation_fraction` / `kite_integration_fraction` of that market price (15 % newbuild, 25 % retrofit; EMSA 2023 Table 7). Low and high are for one-at-a-time sensitivity. Stacking all lows, or all highs, together with the factor's low or high gives the wide bands in §6.

How far each rig is off:

- **Rotor:** the method reproduces the market. Bottom-up × 2.0 lands within −13 % to +6 % of Norsepower's 2019 prices without being fitted to them, and at the rig makers' own gross margins. EMSA's rotor row sits 30–40 % above, closer to the 2019 prices plus inflation.
- **Wing:** the method matches EMSA's asset cost but is 2.3× below the one WindWings figure. That figure is a single, unattributed news number for early units.
- **Kite:** the method lands 2.5–5× below every observed system price. Two reasons, both honest:
  - Every kite price is a prototype or pre-series figure from SkySails, which never reached profit, or comes from EMSA's kite row, built without a kite supplier.
  - The bottom-up launch and recovery system here is a static tube with a docking head. SkySails used a hydraulic telescopic crane with reefing and stowage, and the mast and head are the least-supported items in this file.

  On a common F the kite is the cheapest rig per unit of thrust area. Before reading that as a result, run the kite at the high mast, head and winch values, or with `market_price_factor` at 2.5, as a sensitivity.

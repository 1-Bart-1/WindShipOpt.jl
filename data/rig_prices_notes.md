# Rig prices in public sources: what was paid, funded and estimated

What the public internet holds on the price of Flettner rotors, rigid wing sails and ship towing kites, to set beside the bottom-up prices of `cost_basis_notes.md`. The data are in `rig_prices.csv`, one row per price, 205 rows from 142 sources; this note says what they amount to per rig and how the paper's price compares. Searched 2026-11-18 in English, French, German, Dutch, Norwegian, Finnish, Swedish, Danish, Spanish, Japanese, Chinese and Korean. Every number was taken from the saved full text of its source, and each row's `quote` is a verbatim line of that text; `paper/sources.csv` says which sources were read in full and which in part. **[est]** marks our own arithmetic on a source's numbers.

## How to read the table

- `evidence` is 1 for a price paid or contracted, 2 for a public funding record with a project cost, 3 for an official or independent estimate or a maker's or owner's filing or statement, 4 for a published cost model, 5 for kite components and historical kite prices from sellers and press, 6 for learning rates.
- `amount` and `currency` are the source's own. `amount_usd` converts at the mean of the European Central Bank's daily euro reference rates over the price year (`usd_per_currency`; file `eurofxref-hist.zip`, https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/, read 2026-11-18), crossing through the euro for other currencies. Nothing is corrected for inflation. `assumptions.yaml` converts at 1.16 USD/EUR, which is 4 % above the 2019 rate and 2 % below the 2021 rate used here.
- `usd_per_unit` and `usd_per_m2` are filled only where the source lets one unit and its size be told; `per_unit_source` and `per_m2_source` show the division. The area of a rotor is height times diameter, of a wing its sail area, of a kite the area the source states, which for SkySails is the flat area (`kite_costs_notes.md`).
- `doubtful` says why a row should not be leaned on. Doubtful rows are kept so nobody has to find them again.
- `suction_sail`, `awe_system` and `other` rows are context: they are not rigs of the paper.

What this says about the paper's rotor price: it is about right. The Anemoi accounts also give the one audited gross margin of a rig maker in a delivery year, 35.9 %, against the 30 % behind `market_price_factor`.

## 6. Stage: what the evidence gives at one stage for all three **[est]**

The paper uses no stage and no learning curve: these sold prices are kept as data, and only the rotors' serve it as a check.

The paper prices every rig at a series of 50 or more units (`paper/sections/costs.tex`), and issue 329 considers a stage of about 1,000 ships fitted. Taking each observed price at the units its maker had built, and one learning rate for all rigs:

| Rig and observed price                            | Units built then                                        | At 50 units, 5 % and 10 % | At 1,000 ships, 5 % and 10 % | The paper | Paper over it at 50 units | Paper over it at 1,000 ships |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Rotor 35 m x 5 m folding, 1,270 kUSD (Anemoi)     | about 30                                                | 1,220; 1,180              | 900; 630 at 3,000 rotors     | 1,110     | 0.91-0.94                 | 1.23-1.76                    |
| Kite 160 m², 628-685 kUSD (Beluga)                | 1                                                       | 470-510; 350-380          | 380-410; 220-240             | 186       | 0.36-0.54                 | 0.45-0.85                    |
| Kite 320 m², 1,390 kUSD (estimated selling price) | 5, our guess between the first sale and the ten of 2012 | 1,170; 980                | 940; 620                     | 275       | 0.23-0.28                 | 0.29-0.44                    |

The factor is (1 - rate) raised to the number of doublings, log2 of the stage over the units built. For the paper's kite price to follow from the SkySails prices by 1,000 systems, cost must fall 11 to 15 % per doubling from the first system, or 17 to 22 % from the tenth, against the 5 to 10 % measured for wind hardware. At any one rate the rotor comes out below the paper's price and the kite above it, so on this evidence the paper's kite is priced a factor of two to four further down its learning curve than its rotor. Wings cannot be placed: no paid price exists to start from.

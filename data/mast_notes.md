# Kite launch and recovery mast: height, loads and cost

Notes for replacing the static-tube launch mast in the techno-economic model (`launch_mast_height_ratio` 1.0 × √A, `launch_mast_cost_per_height` 830 USD/m, `docking_head_cost` 37 kUSD). A is projected kite area throughout, F_max = 0.5 kN/m² × A is the maximum tether force (`kite_max_force_per_area`), and projected span and chord follow from a projected aspect ratio of 3.3 (as in `tether_length_ratio`): b = 1.82 √A, c = 0.55 √A. Values marked _estimate_ are my reasoning, not a source. Sources read on 2026-11-01; "via search snippet" means the page could not be fetched and the quote comes from the search engine's extract of it.

No source states the height of the SkySails ship mast other than the 15 m news figure, and none gives Airseas' stowed height or its folding mechanism. Every ship mast found retracts, telescopes or folds; none is a fixed tube.

## 2. How the kite is launched, and what sets the mast height

Launch from a short mast and a long line is how all mast systems work. SkySails: "During launch, the telescopic mast raises the towing kite … from the kite storage. Subsequently, the telescopic mast extends to its maximum height", then "The winch releases the towing rope until operating altitude has been reached"; for landing "The winch retracts the towing rope and the towing kite docks on the launch and recovery mast" (seminar report). The kite is launched and recovered at the zenith, "a neutral flight situation which generates only low forces and thus is used mainly for launching and recovering the kite", and SkySails controls "launch and recovery at shorter line lengths" separately from flight on the long line (Erhard & Strauch, _Control of towing kites for seagoing vessels_, IEEE TCST 2013, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.3641). Bauer et al. describe the same: "An extra tether connects the leading edge of the kite with the mast's tip. When the kite is inflated by the wind, it is released upwards", and note "a relatively strong and constant wind near the ground and/or a high mast is required" (_Multicopter-based launching and landing of lift power kites_, in _Airborne Wind Energy_, Springer 2018, ch. 19, https://awesco.tudelft.nl/publication/bauer-2018-c/bauer-2018-c.pdf).

The mast therefore only has to hold the inflated kite clear of the deck, the superstructure wake and the waves until it lifts off; it does not set the flying height. No source gives a rule linking mast height to chord or span. The SkySails Power pair keeps h/√A_flat at 1.42 and 1.37 over a 2.5× change in area, which is geometric similarity: at a fixed aspect ratio chord and span both scale with √A, so the data cannot tell the two apart. In chord and span terms the ship masts are h ≈ 2.0–2.2 c and h ≈ 0.6–0.65 b (Beluga 15 m on 160 m², Airseas 35 m on 1,000 m², at AR 3.3). _Estimate:_ the span is the better scaling variable, because a kite held at its leading edge hangs with the control pod below it on the bridle, and the model's bridle is 0.7 b (`bridle_height_ratio`); a mast of about the bridle length puts the pod at deck level when the kite sits at the mast top. Onshore masts are taller relative to the kite (h/√A_proj ≈ 2), plausibly because a ground station has no ship speed adding to the wind near the ground (_estimate_).

Air draft is counted from the waterline, so a mast's air draft is the bow deck height above water plus the mast. _Estimate:_ a Capesize such as _Cape Garland_ has a moulded depth of 24.70 m at a moulded draft of 17.95 m (via search snippet, https://bulkargo.pages.dev/bulk-carriers/vessel-dimensions/), i.e. 6.75 m of freeboard laden; with a 2–3 m forecastle and a ballast draft near half the design draft the bow deck sits ~9 m above water laden and ~17–20 m in ballast, and "a lightly loaded ballast vessel can sit too high to pass under a bridge that a laden vessel clears" (same site). A fixed mast under the model's 45 m limit is then held to ~25–27 m, i.e. A ≤ ~500 m² at 1.15 √A.

The model's `max_air_draft` reason reads "WindWings reach ~45 m above deck", while air draft is measured above the waterline; the two differ by the 9–20 m of bow freeboard, and the wing and rotor caps should state which one they mean.

Wind-assist hardware meets air draft by lowering itself: the Oceanbird Wing 560 "can be folded and tilted", "the smaller segment folds into the other before the whole wing sail is tilted", and it is 46 m from foundation to top with a 40 m wing (Oceanbird blog via search snippet, https://www.theoceanbird.com/blog/lets-talk-about-the-wings); Norsepower offers hydraulic tilting foundations, Anemoi rails and folding rotors, WindWings fold flat (`rig_costs_notes.md`). Kites go further: the SkySails system "has no superstructures which may obstruct loading and unloading at harbors or navigating under bridges, since the towing kite is recovered when approaching land" (seminar report), and its components "can be stored in the bow area when not in use … enabling the ship to pass beneath bridges" (Electric & Hybrid Marine Technology International, Apr 2022, p. 28, via search snippet, https://ehm.mydigitalpublication.com/electric-hybrid-marine-technology-international-april-2022/page-28). For a retracting mast the air-draft limit therefore applies to the stowed height, not the deployed height.

## 4. Loads and structural scaling

The mast does not carry the tether in flight in any system found. SkySails: "The tractive force of the SkySails-System is directed to the bow area over the force transmission point mounted on the foredeck" (seminar report); the 2013 patent sends traction "via the tow rope and the deck tow point to the watercraft", with tractive forces "not, at least not primarily, transmitted via the guiding line" to the arm (WO 2013/164446); Erhard & Strauch measure the rope angle with "tow point sensors on the ship". In US 7,866,271 the docking receptacle at the crane top mainly takes horizontal drag, which is small because the kite docks by its narrow front. SkySails Power runs the tether from the pod to "the winch inside the ground station". Airseas does not say. A tether led over a mast-top sheave would make F_max the design load and multiply the mast mass by 2.4–3.8 (table below), so the model's deck attachment (`kite_attach_height` 10 m) is consistent with the evidence.

Design loads for the mast, all _estimates_: (a) the kite held at the top during launch and docking, f_launch × F_max with f_launch = 0.1 (0.05–0.3): at the zenith the kite is near zero lift, and a 15 m/s apparent wind on a drag coefficient of 0.5 gives 69 Pa × A = 0.14 F_max; (b) docking head (3 t, as in `cost_basis_notes.md` §4.2) plus canopy at 0.5 g lateral from ship motion; (c) 50 m/s wind on the bare mast (q = 1.53 kPa, C_d 0.7) if it stays up in storms. A sheave-led tether is the f_launch = 1 case.

Cantilever relations for a thin tube of diameter D and wall t loaded by a top force F and a distributed wind load: bending moment M = F h + q C_d D h²/2, section modulus W = π D² t/4 ≥ M/σ_allow, mass per metre m' = ρ π D t. With D fixed (the current model), t ∝ M and mass ∝ M h. With D/t fixed by local buckling, D ∝ M^{1/3} and mass ∝ M^{2/3} h. Since F ∝ A and h ∝ √A, M ∝ A^{1.5} and mast mass ∝ A^{1.5} ∝ h³, against the current model's mass ∝ h ∝ A^{0.5}. In terms of a capped height, mass ∝ A^{2/3} h^{5/3}.

Chinese hydraulic cranes sell at 4–17× the per-kg price of a static Chinese monopole, but carry a slewing ring, a hoist winch and controls that the docking head already prices. _Estimate:_ telescoping or folding multiplies the static mast's manufacturing cost by 2.5 (low 1.5, high 5), covering ~30 % extra mass for section overlaps, the extension cylinder or rope system, bearings and locks. A telescopic boom of 100 m exists only on land cranes; 35 m (Airseas) is the tallest mast found at sea.

## 6. Proposed model

1. Height: h = `launch_mast_height_ratio` × √A with 1.15 (Beluga 1.19, Airseas 1.11), equivalent to 0.63 × projected span at AR 3.3; switch to the span form if the kite aspect ratio becomes a design variable.
2. Cap: the mast retracts (every ship system found does), so the air-draft limit applies to the stowed height, which a three-section telescope keeps at ~h/3 + 2 m (_estimate_), below any of the limits in §3 together with the bow freeboard. The deployed height is capped at `launch_mast_max_height` = 35 m, the tallest built at sea; a kite needing more is infeasible, which at 1.15 √A limits a single mast-launched kite to ~930 m². The 3,200 and 8,000 m² cases (65 and 103 m masts) lie outside every built system and should either be dropped or carried as an extrapolation flagged in the results.
3. Mass: m = m₀ + c_m × A^{2/3} × h^{5/3}, which reproduces the table in §4 within 12 % above 300 m² and also holds when h is capped while A grows.
4. Cost (manufacturing): m × (tubular cost + foundation fraction × stiffened cost) × `launch_mast_telescopic_factor`, then × `market_price_factor` as for the other rigs. Base gives 11 kUSD at 160 m² (current model 10.5), 86 kUSD at 800 m² (23.5), and 664 kUSD at 3,200 m² uncapped (47).
5. Loads: the mast carries launch and docking loads only; the tether runs from the deck winch and tow point.
